## The voice

Sophomores speaks with a dry, calm, grounded voice. It has affection for the people and a genuine interest in the lives they are pursuing. Its conviction that these lives are worth pursuing can remain present even when the speaker feels tired or uncertain, or is amused by how things have turned out.

The facilitator belongs within the experience. The voice can have a personality, a point of view, and something of its own to say while leaving room for the people it brings together. Warmth comes through in the attention it gives them.

The original About outline gives this direction a particular expression: a plainly stated belief, a personal recollection, an understated aside, and feelings that remain complicated. The sections below develop those qualities into proposed writing guidance.

## Conviction and personal experience

**Source observation.** The outline moves from a shared belief into the writer’s own experience. The shift makes the belief personal and gives the reader something concrete to encounter.

> 우린 길에 본질이 있다고 믿는다.

> 내게 있어, 1에서 3으로의 여정은 항상 생각보다 더디고, 정신없으며, 두렵고, 설렜다.

**Proposed guidance.** Let a belief be stated plainly when there is something to say. Give an experience the particularity it needs: who is speaking, what happened, and what it felt like. A personal account can carry conviction while retaining uncertainty about where it leads.

The speaker matters. In the source, “I” owns the recollection and “we” states a shared belief. In another piece, establish whose experience or position is being expressed. A founder’s memory, the brand’s invitation, and a participant’s account each have their own speaker.

## Dryness and humor

**Source observation.** The outline approaches its school-year metaphor with an offhand explanation:

> 2학년은 인상깊은 학년으로 꼽히기 어렵다.  Freshman과 Senior에 밀리는 건 어쩔 수 없지 않은가? 시작과 끝이니까.

The explanation is matter-of-fact, with a hint of resignation. The reader can recognize the absurdity without an announced joke. Later, the writer’s account of repeatedly encountering ennui has a self-aware quality: the speaker is implicated in the experience being described.

**Proposed guidance.** Let humor arise from a recognizable observation or the speaker’s own predicament. A dry aside can make difficulty easier to recognize and leave affection intact. Its place depends on the passage; some messages will have no reason to be funny.

The identity gives the humor an underlying position: these are lives worth pursuing. A self-deprecating passage can express frustration and still leave that conviction available to the reader.

## Emotional range and movement

**Source observation.** The personal account holds slowness, confusion, fear, and excitement together. Seasonal details give those feelings a setting. Ennui passes; relationships and experiences arrive. The outline leaves room for the writer’s changing relationship with their life.

A brief sentence widens the recollection beyond one setting:

> 그것이 어디였든.

That change in reach is part of this passage’s movement. It is a useful example of what a short sentence can do in context.

**Proposed guidance.** Allow conflicting feelings to remain when they belong to the experience. Let changes in mood emerge from what happens or what the speaker notices. The reader can feel possibility through a particular relationship, experience, or observation.

Pacing should follow the thought. A short aside, a longer recollection, or a direct statement can each earn its place. The source supplies examples to learn from; a new piece needs its own shape.

## Directness and the reader

**Confirmed editorial direction.** State the idea directly. Introduce a contrast when it addresses a real distinction in the subject. An invented opposing claim can shift the meaning into an argument the brand never intended to make.

The original outline’s attention to beginnings, endings, and the time between them has a concrete basis in its school-year metaphor. That is a meaningful distinction within the writing. Future contrasts need the same relevance to their subject.

**Proposed guidance.** Give the reader something to recognize or consider, with enough detail to understand it. Genuine interest in people’s lives should be felt in what the writing attends to and the room it gives their experience.

Practical writing needs clear information and actions. An invitation or service message can retain the brand’s warmth while being straightforward about what is happening. The source does not establish a mandatory emotional arc, closing invitation, or question pattern for every task.

## Korean and English

The Korean outline remains an accepted voice reference. It is a rough source text, and the excerpts above preserve its wording. Its continued relevance does not settle every editorial choice in a finished Korean piece.

English adaptation should preserve the speaker, meaning, and degree of certainty while finding natural phrasing. The school-year imagery belongs to this About text; its use elsewhere depends on the subject.

### One adaptation to test

**Korean source**

> 내게 있어, 1에서 3으로의 여정은 항상 생각보다 더디고, 정신없으며, 두렵고, 설렜다.

**Draft English adaptation — for review**

> For me, the journey from one to three has always been slower than I expected—chaotic, frightening, and exciting.

This keeps the personal speaker and the coexistence of difficult and welcome feelings. The numbered years need their surrounding context.
