### Schema (the intent collection only)

**Grant fields:**
- `signer` (text, indexed): the signer's CAIP-10 account, `eip155:<chain>:<address>`;
- `nonce` (number);
- `sends` (json): every signed transaction, as `{ txHash, maxFeePerGas, maxPriorityFeePerGas, sentAt }`;
- `lastSentAt` (date, indexed);
- `settledTxHash` (text).

**Cancel fields:** `cancelAttemptID`, `cancelStartedAt`, `cancelSigner` (indexed), `cancelNonce`, `cancelTxHash`, `cancelSubmittedAt`.

**Hook rules added:**
- **An attempt's record is fixed.**
  - Its `signer` and `nonce` never change.
  - `sends` is append-only, and its first entry must be the intent's `txHash`. Entries are compared field by field, because stored JSON comes back with its keys reordered.
  - Only a proven-nonexecution retry (`failed_retryable → pending`) clears these fields.
- **`settledTxHash`** must be one of the hashes the intent recorded.
- **`granted → cancelled`** needs `cancelRequested`, `cancelledAt`, and either a recorded `cancelTxHash` or the code `key_not_valid`.
- **A recorded `cancelTxHash` never changes.** It is cleared only together with its attempt ID, nonce and signer, which is how an attempt proven unsent is cleared.

## 1. Signer serialisation across processes

- **The lock.**
  - Nonce choice, the write-ahead record and the broadcast all run while the dispatching process holds `pg_advisory_xact_lock` on `commerce:key-signer:<CAIP-10>`.
  - The lock is taken through the existing `lockCommerce` helper, in a transaction of its own (`inCommerceTransaction`). So the write-ahead record still commits in its own short transaction before the broadcast.
  - The CAIP-10 account names both the signer and the chain, so the lock is per signer and chain.
- **The nonce floor.**
  - The adapter's nonce is the largest of three values: the chain's `pending` count; the in-process lane; and the worker's floor.
  - The floor is one past the highest nonce this signer has in flight in the database: grants `submitted` or `ambiguous` with a nonce, and cancels that were sent and have not settled.
  - The maximum is computed in code, comparing numbers, not by a sorted query.
- **A second check.**
  - The write-ahead transaction re-checks, under a second short lock (`key-signer-nonce:`), that no other in-flight intent already holds the chosen nonce.
  - If one does, nothing is broadcast, and the claim goes to `failed_retryable` with `nonce_conflict`.
  - This holds even if the outer lock's connection were lost.
- **If the lock cannot be taken** after the claim, the claim is recorded at once as proven not sent (`signer_lock_unavailable`). It is not left for the ten-minute sweep.
- **One adapter rule changed.**
  - Before, the adapter reused a nonce after an unclear broadcast that the node turned out not to hold.
  - That is removed. A nonce recorded on an intent stays with that intent until it settles. Otherwise a later replacement of the first intent could overwrite another intent's grant.
  - The gap is filled instead by replacing the stuck intent at its own nonce. The adapter test changed from `[0, 0]` to `[0, 1]` and failed first.
- **Timeouts.** `src/payload.config.ts` sets no `statement_timeout` or `idle_in_transaction_session_timeout`, so the lock holder is not cut off while it waits on RPC. The proof database sets both to 15 s, and the dispatchers there finish well inside that.

## 2. Stuck-grant replacement

- **The pass.** `replaceStuckKeyGrants({ payload, adapter })` is explicitly invoked, like the other passes.
  - It selects this signer's `submitted` and `ambiguous` grants whose last send (`lastSentAt`) is older than `REPLACE_AFTER_MS`, which is 10 minutes.
  - It does not use reconcile's `nextAttemptAt`, so the two passes never reschedule each other.
- **Same nonce, higher fee.**
  - The adapter re-signs the same `grantKeys` call at the recorded nonce.
  - Each of `maxFeePerGas` and the tip becomes the larger of 1.25 times its previous value and the network's current estimate.
  - Both must rise strictly and by at least 10%, geth's price bump.
  - The fee cap may not pass the chain's ceiling. `maxFeeCeiling[84532]` is 0.05 gwei, ten times Base Sepolia's floor base fee.
  - First sends above the ceiling are refused too, with `fee_above_ceiling`.
  - The expiry is recomputed from the lock's duration at the moment of re-signing.
- **Never a new nonce while an older transaction could mine.**
  - Before re-signing, the adapter reads the `latest` nonce count. If the chain already counts the nonce as used, nothing is signed (`nonceConsumed`) and reconcile settles it.
  - A replacement that fails in any way only sets a `replacement_*` code. This covers a prepare that is refused or throws, and a broadcast that is refused, throws or is unclear.
  - The state never leaves `submitted` or `ambiguous`, so the intent is never dispatched again at a new nonce.
- **Write-ahead.** Each replacement's hash, fee and time are appended to `sends` in a committed transaction, under the signer lock, before it is broadcast.
- **Reconcile against every hash.**
  - `reconcileKeyGrants` checks `txHash` and every entry in `sends`.
  - A confirmed hash grants the key. A reverted one proves the nonce consumed, and the intent goes to `failed_retryable`.
  - `settledTxHash` records which hash it was.
- **The cap.**
  - `MAX_REPLACEMENTS` is 3. Once the last replacement is past its deadline, the intent goes to `ambiguous` with `replacement_exhausted`.
  - When no raise fits under the ceiling, it goes to `ambiguous` with `replacement_fee_ceiling`.
  - Both keep being reconciled. Reconcile's 30-minute `confirmation_overdue` never overwrites these two codes.
- **Older rows.** Rows recorded before this slice have no `signer`, `nonce` or `sends`. They are never replaced and keep the old behaviour.

## 3. Cancel on chain (ADR-0009)

- **The pass.** `cancelKeyGrants({ payload, adapter })` takes a `KeyCancelAdapter` built on the **lock-manager** key, through `createUnlockKeyCancelAdapter` and `loadLockManagerAccount`. It is explicitly invoked, with no scheduler. Each pass:
  1. sweeps cancel claims interrupted before a hash was recorded;
  2. reconciles sent cancels from their receipts;
  3. claims and sends new ones.
- **Idempotent, and sent at most once.**
  - The claim (`cancelAttemptID`) is taken under the intent's lock.
  - The hash, nonce and signer are recorded before the broadcast, under the manager's signer lock.
  - A recorded cancel is never sent again, whatever its broadcast answered.
  - It ends `cancelled`, with `cancelledAt` and `cancelTxHash`.
  - A key that is already invalid on chain is recorded `cancelled` with `key_not_valid`, and no transaction is sent.
- **Never the grant signer.** Three checks:
  - The cancel has its own interface and adapter.
  - The worker parks the intent (`cancel_signer_is_granter`) if the cancel adapter's account is the intent's grant signer.
  - Before signing, the adapter reads `isLockManager(account)` on chain and refuses with `not_lock_manager`. The grant signer is not a lock manager (ADR-0008); the live run read that back as `false`.
- **A late grant.** A grant still in flight when the refund flags it is reconciled to `granted`, with its token, first. The next cancel pass then cancels it.
- **Parked cancel codes.** These wait for a person:
  - `not_lock_manager`;
  - `token_mismatch` (the token's owner is not the intent's recipient);
  - `invalid_token` and `invalid_target`;
  - `chain_not_enabled`;
  - `cancel_signer_is_granter`;
  - `cancel_reverted`.

  A cancel still unanswered after 30 minutes gets `cancel_overdue` and keeps being reconciled.

### Which PublicLock v14 function, and why

The cancel uses `expireAndRefundFor(tokenId, 0)`, read from `@unlock-protocol/contracts/dist/PublicLock/PublicLockV14.sol` (MixinRefunds).
- **Only a lock manager may call it** (`_onlyLockManager`). That is ADR-0008's separate cancel authority.
- **The refund is an explicit zero.** The KRW refund is PortOne's (ADR-0009).
- **It expires the key at the block's timestamp** (`_cancelKey`) and keeps the ownership record. The token and its owner stay readable as evidence.
- **It zeroes `_originalDurations` and `_originalPrices`**, so the key can never renew.
- **It emits `CancelKey(tokenId, owner, sendTo, refund)`.** Reconcile matches exactly one such log, from the lock, for this token, with a refund of 0, at the chain's depth.

The alternatives, and why not:
- **`cancelAndRefund`** can also be called by the key's owner, its key manager or an approved address. It pays a refund computed from the lock's price, out of the lock.
- **`setKeyExpiration`** emits only `ExpirationChanged` and leaves the key renewable.
- **`burn`** destroys the ownership record, and it is a transfer subject to the lock's transfer rules.

Two consequences:
- It reverts with `KEY_NOT_VALID` on a key that is already invalid, so `isValidKey` is read first.
- `totalKeys(recipient)` stays at 1 or more afterwards. A later grant to the same recipient therefore parks as `recipient_has_key` for a person.

## Not met / open points

- **The live replacement did not show a pooled low-fee transaction being displaced.**
  - The node accepted the low-fee send, but did not know it 20 s later.
  - From outside I cannot tell whether the sequencer pooled it and then dropped it, or never pooled it.
  - The replacement itself is proven: same nonce, higher fee, it mined, and reconcile settled on it.
- **A cancel has no replacement.**
  - Suppose a cancel's hash is recorded but never reaches the node; for example, the process stops between the write-ahead record and the broadcast.
  - The cancel then stays unknown. After 30 minutes it gets `cancel_overdue` and waits for a person; it is never sent again.
  - Grants have the replacement pass for this case. Cancels would need the same pass, at the same nonce.
- **A stuck nonce blocks the ones after it.**
  - A grant that has used all its replacements keeps its nonce in flight. Later grants from that signer queue behind it until a person acts.
  - Never handing that nonce to another intent is deliberate.
- **The in-flight nonce scan is bounded** at 500 rows per signer. That is far above any expected backlog, but it is still a bound.
- **Polygon (137) has no fee ceiling yet.** Its enablement slice sets one. Every chain except 84532 is still refused.
- **The live proofs use the in-memory intent store**, as the adapter slice's did. The database behaviour is proven separately, against PostgreSQL.
