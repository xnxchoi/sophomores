## Copy

Second year rarely stands out. Freshman and senior year have the obvious advantage of being a beginning and an end. 

Yet trying to remember them feels like trying to recall a dream.

Freshman years, it was always around summer: the heat would bring a state of dull, dreamy trance, clawing into me. Falls were spent in ennui; the deep sense of dread would haunt me into winter, disappearing as new years came to a head.

Senior years were a different kind of haze. Rushed from one task to the next until there wasn’t any more left but to wait. A peculiar peace—a lulled anticipation and dread, unable to hasten nor delay what is to come.

Nowadays, I’m not sure the year even has a number. 

Some days you’re wandering around, completely out of your depth. Other days you’re waiting on a curtain call without the faintest idea what the date is. 

Nothing starts, and nothing ends, until you say so. 

That was always the real terror behind the ennui: agency. 

When you build a life on your own terms, you lose the luxury of an alibi. What you’re chasing is real enough to taste, but if your grip slips, it doesn't crash—it just evaporates. The room stays quiet. Nobody files a report. And even when you find your footing, that dread never quite retires. It waits on a quiet Tuesday, asking who you think you’re kidding.

And yet, almost everything I cherish belongs somewhere in between. 

You know the feeling. You’ve been trapped inside your own head for weeks, running the same calculations until they blur. Then someone asks what you’re working on, and does something disarming: they actually wait for the answer. 

Not the elevator pitch—just genuine curiosity. 

In trying to explain the mess of it out loud, you suddenly remember what pulled you in. Through their eyes, the thing looks alive again. That excitement stays behind; it lingers like an ember until morning.

And the relief runs both ways. You take the spotlight off yourself and become an invested audience for someone else's obsession. Their pursuit might look nothing like yours, but you find yourself genuinely giving a damn where it goes. 

That is what Sophomores is for. 

A room for people building lives of their own. Somewhere to step away, pick up a thread where you left it six months ago, and meet someone new. You spend an evening caught up in other people’s worlds, and you walk out with your appetite back—and with people who make you want to see what happens next.

Start lines are arbitrary. Finish lines are an invention. The unmeasured distance between them is the only place anyone actually lives.

Walking your own path doesn’t mean you have to walk alone.
