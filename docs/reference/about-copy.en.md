Second year rarely stands out. Freshman and senior year have the obvious advantage of being a beginning and an end.

Remembering those years feels like trying to recall a dream. 

Freshman years, it was always around summer: the heat would bring a state of dull, dreamy trance, clawing into me. Falls were spent in ennui; the deep sense of dread would haunt me into winter, disappearing as new years came to a head.

Senior years were a different kind of haze. Rushed from one task to the next until there wasn't any more left but to wait. A peculiar peace- a lulled anticipation and dread, unable to hasten nor delay what is to come.

Some days it’s hard to tell where things stand at all. Still first year, perhaps—lost, wandering, none the wiser. It's harder to tell when and how, but curtain call is eventually bound to come: 

Beginnings and endings often forced me to stare eye-level into this morbid proposition. "Before him lay a huge, dark abyss that ran straight through to the earth’s core. All he could see was a thick cloud of nothingness swirling around him; all he could hear was a profound silence squeezing his eardrums."


Now I realize my ennui always had its signs- a manifestation of fear of _agency_. The terror of  what is almost real - enough to leave a taste on your tongue- can fade irrecoverably into nonexistence, all at the _slip_ of your grip.
