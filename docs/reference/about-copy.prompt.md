# Prompt — an independent English About for Sophomores

Copy the prompt below into a fresh LLM chat and attach the source packet, or point a repository-enabled agent at these files. With the ZIP, extract it and provide access to the files inside; the folder structure preserves their relative links.

Required files:

- `docs/reference/about-copy.en.md`

---

Write a new English About piece for Sophomores. Read the attached editorial handoff and all five source documents before drafting. The handoff explains the retained writing, unresolved material, explicit user decisions, and why previous attempts failed. If you cannot access a required file, say which one rather than inventing its contents.

This is an English original with a Korean counterpart, not a translation. Match the underlying content and emotional stakes while writing naturally in English. Do not imitate the Korean sentence or paragraph structure.

The current English file is an incomplete, user-edited working checkpoint. Some prose has been retained; other passages are exploratory, and braced sections describe what remains to be written. Do not treat all of it as finished copy. Use the opening anchors identified in the handoff as your strongest evidence of desired direction and prefer retaining them; clearly identify consequential revisions. Preserve the explicitly retained closing. Develop and restructure the unfinished body as needed. The last rewrite was rejected for direction and prose; the previous assistant's explanation of why is a hypothesis, not a user-confirmed diagnosis.

The piece should feel reflective, colloquial, personal, and emotionally resonant, while leaving room for the reader within it. Occasional first person and conversational second person are both available. Do not make every sentence a confession or remove the personal experience in pursuit of an impersonal voice. Narrative distance must not become obscurity.

Carry the school-year framing into recurring life experiences: beginnings and endings can feel detached, while the middle contains much of what is cherished. Self-directed pursuits bring uncertainty and a sense of fragility. Conversations with people who are genuinely interested can renew excitement, and their lives become worth following too. Sophomores makes room for those encounters and for returning to them; participants bring the care and interest.

Give the affirmative experiences at least as much emotional attention as the darker material, probably more. That does not mean producing an uplifting recovery story. Unease need not be solved. The pleasure, curiosity, and excitement need to feel lived, rather than be described as community benefits. Write the experience the notes point toward instead of paraphrasing the notes.

Avoid translation-shaped English, invented opposing claims, cryptic fragments, generic encouragement, overexplained metaphors, unsupported biography, and promises that community fixes people's lives. Do not use the unverified quotation in the working draft. The references to another show's narration concern narrative distance, not stylistic imitation.

Return **one complete candidate first**, then a brief note on consequential choices or remaining uncertainties. Do not begin with an analysis, a menu of approaches, a questionnaire, or a claim that the writing succeeds. There is no prescribed word count; make the length earn its place.

If working in the repository, save the candidate separately and report its path. Do not overwrite the existing English checkpoint, modify the Korean or brand references, commit, publish, push, deploy, or call paid external models. In a chat-only setting, present the candidate directly.
